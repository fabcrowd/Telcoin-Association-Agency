## STRATEGIC RATIONALE

TANIP-1 is the first formal governance proposal type executed at the application layer of the Telcoin platform. A crypto-native audience will recognize the governance parallels (Ethereum EIPs, Cosmos governance proposals) but likely has no context for what the TAN layer is, why it needs its own proposal type, or what TANIP-1 actually did. The thread establishes: (1) what a TANIP is and where it sits in the governance hierarchy, (2) what TANIP-1 specifically governed, and (3) why the outcome is meaningful - including the honest finding that TANIP-1 revealed mercenary capital behavior that prompted a direct governance response.

This is an Education post. One institutional conversation prompt closes the thread.

### Tweet 2 of 3 — What TANIP-1 actually was

**Text:**

TANIP-1 established an on-chain staking and referral rewards program: stakers lock TEL, refer users, and earn a share of trading fees - up to 42% of referred users' fees depending on staked amount.

3.2 million TEL was distributed weekly across participants.

The program ran, the computation worked. But the data showed fee activity converging on the issuance amount - fees generated by cycling capital through the protocol, not organic demand. Wallet growth was flat. Referral flows were often circular.

The governance system identified the problem and responded directly.

**Character count:** ~490 (split across one tweet as close to 280 as the content allows - this should be split to stay within limit; see note below)

Post-program analysis found fee activity converging around the issuance amount. Wallets generated fees by cycling capital through the protocol. Organic demand was not the driver. Active wallet counts remained flat; referral flows were often self-referential.

### Tweet 3 (or 4) of 3 — Governance response + conversation prompt

**Text:**

The TAN Council responded with a replacement model: the Trading Fee Rebate Program. The only structural change is a cap - a wallet cannot earn more TEL than it actually paid in fees during that period.

The formula: rebate = min(calculated issuance, TEL fees paid). The infrastructure from TANIP-1 remains in place.

How does this compare to how other L1 ecosystems have addressed mercenary liquidity in governance-controlled incentive programs?
