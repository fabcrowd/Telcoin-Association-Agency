**Tweet 1/6 — Hook: What is TAN and where does it sit?**

The Telcoin Platform has three layers.

Telcoin Network is the settlement infrastructure. TELx is the liquidity layer. TAN — the Telcoin Application Network — is the application layer.

TAN is where the infrastructure built in the layers below becomes accessible to end users and builders. It is the surface where a mobile user makes a transaction, a developer deploys an application, and a staker earns rewards for bringing people onto the platform.

This post covers TAN: what it is, how it works, and why its incentive model is different from most application networks.

---

**Tweet 2/6 — TAN architecture**

TAN is not a separate blockchain. It is the application network that sits on top of Telcoin Network and TELx, inheriting their settlement security and liquidity infrastructure.

The primary application built on TAN today is the Telcoin Wallet, available on iOS and Android. TAN is also an open developer environment — EVM-compatible, fully open-source, and accessible to any builder through the Telcoin Association GitHub.

The TAN Council (six elected members) governs the application layer through the TANIP process: TAN Improvement Proposals. Every major TAN policy change — reward models, developer allocations, parameter adjustments — goes through a governance vote before implementation.

---

**Tweet 3/6 — Proof of Alignment**

TAN's incentive mechanism is called Proof of Alignment.

The core principle: rewards are tied to measurable adoption activity, not passive token holding.

TAN participants — called stakers — deposit TEL and earn through two channels:

1. Referral fees: Up to 42% of the trading fees generated by referred users, earned in real time as those users transact.

2. Weekly TEL issuance: 3.2 million TEL distributed per week across all stakers based on adoption activity — not simply by size of stake.

The mechanism is designed to create alignment between the network's growth and the economic interests of participants. A staker benefits most when referred users are genuinely active. Activity drives rewards. Holding alone does not.

---

**Tweet 4/6 — Builder ecosystem**

TAN is not a single application. It is a network meant to support many.

Three projects currently in development within the TAN builder ecosystem:

- Telcoin Name Service: Human-readable .tel addresses (e.g. alice.tel) — an ENS equivalent native to the Telcoin platform, designed to integrate directly into wallet UX.

- Charity NFT Subscription: Revokable NFTs tied to streaming on-chain donations — NFT is held while funds stream to a charity wallet and revoked when the stream stops. Full on-chain transparency.

- Lottery micro-app: A no-loss game currently live on Base, with Chainlink VRF integration planned. It will migrate to Telcoin Network at mainnet.

Developer incentives — a future TANIP specifically designed to reward GSMA-member application development on TAN — are in development. Current builders can build on Base now and redeploy to Telcoin Network at mainnet without architecture changes.

Codebase: github.com/Telcoin-Association

---

**Tweet 5/6 — The end-to-end stack**

Three posts in this series have covered the three layers separately. Here is how they connect.

A user opens the Telcoin Wallet (TAN layer) and initiates a transaction.

The transaction routes through TELx (liquidity layer), where AMM pools provide the on-chain liquidity and price discovery needed to complete the exchange.

Settlement is recorded on Telcoin Network (infrastructure layer), validated by GSMA mobile network operator nodes.

Each layer contributes something the others cannot: TAN provides the user interface and distribution surface. TELx provides the liquidity and exchange mechanism. Telcoin Network provides the settlement and security.

The three layers are not interchangeable. They are designed to function together.
