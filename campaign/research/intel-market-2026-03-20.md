### 1. OCC Issues Proposed GENIUS Act Implementation Rules — Comment Period Closes May 1

**Headline**: The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) issued a notice of proposed rulemaking on February 25, 2026, to implement the GENIUS Act. The proposal covers application requirements, permissible activities, reserve maintenance obligations, and a notable prohibition on payment of interest or yield by stablecoin issuers. The FDIC separately extended its own GENIUS Act comment period to May 18, 2026.

**What it means for Telcoin**: eUSD (issued by Telcoin Digital Asset Bank under a Nebraska state charter) is positioned inside the exact regulatory structure the GENIUS Act is now operationalizing. The OCC's "no yield" prohibition on payment stablecoins reinforces eUSD's compliance posture and distinguishes it from yield-bearing stablecoin competitors who will face structural constraints. The FDIC extension signals regulators are moving carefully — giving Telcoin's banking team more runway to finalize operations ahead of the July 18, 2026 final rule deadline.

**Category**: Content opportunity — reinforces the "first regulated bank-issued stablecoin" narrative. A thread explaining how eUSD is already structured for GENIUS Act compliance would be timely and differentiating. Institutional audience appropriate.

### 2. HSBC and Standard Chartered Set to Receive Hong Kong's First Stablecoin Licenses

**Headline**: Per Bloomberg reporting covered by Disruption Banking (March 13–15, 2026), HSBC and Standard Chartered are expected to be among Hong Kong's inaugural cohort of licensed stablecoin issuers, with HKMA targeting "a very small number" of licenses this month. The licenses are granted under the Stablecoins Ordinance (effective August 1, 2025), which requires 100% reserve backing and daily reserve disclosure.

**What it means for Telcoin**: Hong Kong's decision to prioritize note-issuing banks signals that institutional credibility is a core criterion for stablecoin legitimacy — a frame that also validates Telcoin's bank-charter-first approach in the U.S. This is a parallel data point to the Nebraska charter milestone. The 36-application funnel being narrowed to 3–4 licenses illustrates how high the regulatory bar is, reinforcing the scarcity value of Telcoin's existing charter.

**Category**: Content opportunity — use as proof of a broader global pattern: regulators worldwide are concluding that only institutionally credentialed entities should issue stablecoins. Positions eUSD not as crypto-native but as part of a regulated international wave.

### 3. Stablecoin Transaction Volume Surpassed $47.6 Trillion in 2025 — Sub-Saharan Africa Up 180% YoY

**Headline**: Stablecoin transaction volume more than doubled in 2025 to $47.6 trillion globally, per Crypto.com Research. DeFi volume on L2 networks now represents over 40% of total Ethereum-ecosystem activity, with the cost advantage of L2s specifically cited as meaningful for retail users in emerging markets.

**What it means for Telcoin**: This data validates Telcoin's core thesis at scale. The Sub-Saharan Africa growth rate is directly adjacent to Telcoin's MNO validator network — if MNOs in that region activate as validators, they plug into an already-accelerating stablecoin demand curve. The stat is also a credibility anchor for the financial inclusion narrative.

**Category**: Content opportunity — a post anchored to the $47.6 trillion figure and the Africa growth stat, framed around the infrastructure required to serve these users (hint: MNO distribution + bank-backed stablecoins), would perform well with both crypto-native and institutional audiences.

Wise's continued growth on transparency reinforces that fee structure and exchange rate clarity are primary consumer decision factors — areas where on-chain stablecoin settlement should outperform.

**Category**: Threat to monitor, framing opportunity. Western Union's infrastructure pivot should inform Telcoin's competitive messaging: Telcoin does not compete with Western Union's agent network — it replaces the need for one by routing through telecom subscriber relationships instead.
